# Things Not Accessioned

In Dinosaur Hall I lift a mitten with the litter tongs.
Its thumb points north, past the tail of the plaster beast.
A name runs out of thread halfway across the cuff.
I put it on the trolley beside my cup of tea.
Above us, ribs hold up a patch of painted weather.
I wheel the bucket under their enormous shade.

In the Navigation Gallery a folded map waits on a bench.
Someone has circled toilets, then the exit, then toilets again.
The crease goes straight through a picture of the sun.
I smooth it with the side of my rubber glove.
My trolley squeaks where the floor changes from oak to stone.
The ships stay tilted in their separate glassy storms.

In the Egyptian Court I find a crown made out of paper.
One yellow point is soft with being chewed.
The elastic has a knot too small for my thick fingers.
I lay it on the map, beyond the circled sun.
The floor machine goes round the feet of a seated king.
I reverse it where his toes come through the dark.

At the staff door I arrange the three things on the shelf:
the mitten cupping nothing, the map, the flattened crown.
Tomorrow someone small will drag a sleeve along this counter.
Someone tall will say they have already checked at home.
I tip the cold tea down the sink and rinse the cup.
Then turn the mitten so the stitched name faces out.
