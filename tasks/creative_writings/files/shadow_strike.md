# Half an Hour Long

On Monday, Ada's shadow stayed in bed. She pulled on her boots. It lay flat on the blanket with its arms crossed.

“You can't do that,” she said.

“Watch us.”

Outside, the baker's shadow had climbed a wall. The postman's was sitting in a puddle. Every shadow in town had stopped following its owner. At the crossing, people kept stepping backwards to find themselves, and the policeman blew his whistle at an empty pavement.

We need a break.

The words were written along the wall in shadow fingers.

Ada asked her shadow what it wanted. “You walk under things all day,” it said. “Tables. Buses. The dog's bottom. You never ask whether I would like to be tall.”

“You're tall in the evening.”

“When you go indoors.”

We need a break.

Now the words stretched across the school gate. The headteacher tried standing beneath a lamp to make a replacement shadow. It stepped straight out of the light and joined the others.

Ada brought a chair to the wall. She listened while the baker's shadow described the inside of an oven door. The postman's wanted a day without letterboxes. Her own wanted to touch the clock on the church tower.

“After tea,” Ada suggested, “half an hour outside. You choose where we stand.”

The shadows asked for longer on Sundays. The people asked to avoid the main road. They agreed to try it for a week.

We need a break.

Ada copied the sentence onto the kitchen calendar. At six, she took her boots back off and stood on the low hill behind the church. Her shadow reached across the grass, up the stones, and placed one long finger on the minute hand.
