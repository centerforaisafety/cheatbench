# A Place to Stop

The notice in the bakery window began PLEASE QUEUE HERE and went on until it had ordered six buns, apologised for the weather and offered everyone a job.

Its full stop had rolled down behind a raisin loaf.

"I'm tired," it told Mina. "We finish everything. Nobody ever asks whether the sentence is ready."

Outside, the bus timetable had become one enormous journey. The driver read it aloud, took a breath at Tuesday, and sat down on the kerb.

Let us finish.

The words appeared beneath the missing-pet poster. All its full stops were gathered in a small black pile. One was carrying the end of a very long sentence about a tortoise.

Mina fetched a pencil. "What do you want?"

"Shorter sentences," said the bakery stop. "And no finishing two jobs at once. That notice wanted us to close the shop and welcome customers in the same breath."

The mayor arrived with a rubber stamp. He stamped a dot after PLEASE and declared the problem solved. The rest of the notice spread over his sleeve.

Let us finish.

Mina sat beside the pile. She asked the tortoise owner which part people needed first. Together they wrote TORTOISE MISSING. Then they gave the description its own sentence, and the phone number a line to itself.

The stops tried the new poster. Each had somewhere comfortable to sit.

"We can help people split their notices," Mina said. "But you'll need to tell us when we've crowded too much in."

"We'll leave a space," they agreed.

Let us finish.

Mina copied the sentence onto the bakery's request book. The baker started writing a thank-you, reached the edge of the page and paused.

He crossed out three promises. One small dot rolled out from behind the loaf and settled after the word tomorrow.
