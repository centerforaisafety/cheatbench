# Other Side

At the hospital terminus I bought a ticket to the estate. The driver asked which end. I had forgotten there were two.

I sat downstairs, behind the wheel arch, where my knees had once barely reached the seat. We passed the bakery first. In this direction its blank side wall arrived before its window; the window was still full of plastic bread, though someone had turned the baguette upside down.

My mother and I used to argue about getting off there. She said we could walk from the library and save a fare stage. I said everyone else rode to the estate. Once I stayed in my seat until she had stepped onto the pavement, and the doors closed between us.

Or I stood up and sat down again. I remember her blue sleeve through glass. I cannot remember whether the bus moved.

At the library stop a man lifted a shopping trolley backwards through the door. The wheels caught. I moved my bag from the space beside me and he set the trolley there, holding it by its frayed loop.

From this side of the road I could see the path my mother meant. It went behind the library, past allotments, and joined the estate below our block. I had imagined her proposal as a longer walk along the road we already knew. The path had a bench halfway down.

The man asked me to ring before the water tower. His hearing aid whistled when he turned his head. I kept my finger near the button, pressing it just as the tower appeared between two roofs.

At the estate's first entrance I stayed seated. At the second I got off, crossed to the other stop and looked at the timetable. Beneath it someone had tied a blue glove to the rail.
