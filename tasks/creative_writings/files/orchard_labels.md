## 1. Ceremonial spade, 1924

The mayor used this silver-edged spade to plant what the council described as Bracken Common's first fruit tree. Its blade shows no soil wear. A photograph records the ceremony beside a mature trunk, identified in the original caption as a temporary decorative prop.

## 2. Jam jar, 1911

A paper label on this jar reads Bracken Apples, Second Boiling. The donor attributed it to her grandmother's kitchen. Chemical traces confirm fruit preserve but cannot identify where the fruit grew. The handwritten date predates the council's account of the orchard's establishment by thirteen years.

## 3. Rent book, 1908

Six households paid a small annual charge for gathering windfalls on the common. The collector described the charge as grazing rent, although no animals appear beside these entries. Later pages have been removed. We cannot determine whether the payments continued after the land changed ownership.

## 4. Pruning hook, 1919

The wooden handle carries three sets of initials, each cut over an older mark. Tool specialist Mara Pell identified repeated repairs to the blade socket. Council records list no orchard workers before 1924; the hook's association with the common rests on an unsigned donation note.

## 5. School exercise sheet, 1925

A pupil copied the sentence Our Mayor Gave Us Our Trees across this page. Beneath the final line, a different hand added Grandad grafted them. The school register records two children with the same name. Neither family has been securely linked to the rent book.

## 6. Woven picking basket, 1972

Repairs made with blue parcel cord hold its base together. The donor called it the ceremony basket, but its dimensions differ from the basket in the mayoral photograph. Inside is an undated seed packet marked For the next ones. No variety or planting place is recorded.
