# Ten Past Three

[A station jingle ends on a wavering trumpet.]

MARA: Three o'clock. You're listening to the only programme awake enough to mispronounce your dedication.

DEV: Caller, you're through.

CALLER: At ten past, Dev will say, “That's my mother's cup.” Then you'll hear glass breaking.

DEV: It's ceramic, actually. And I haven't mentioned it on air.

MARA: Somebody from the day shift, surely.

CALLER: Please move it away from the desk.

[A mug scrapes across wood.]

DEV: Done. Floor. Behind my chair. Anything else from the future?

CALLER: Don't let me go.

MARA: We have a record scheduled. Stay on the line; we'll leave your channel open to us.

[Music rises, then fades under a clock announcement.]

MARA: Three minutes past three. Our caller is still holding. Dev has put the mug in a padded envelope, for anyone keeping score at home.

DEV: My sister sent it after the house clearance. I would appreciate an explanation.

CALLER: I only heard the recording.

MARA: What recording?

CALLER: The one they keep playing afterwards.

[The telephone connection crackles.]

DEV: Seven past. I'm taking it out of the room. No mug, no prophecy. Cover the weather for me.

MARA: Dev, the cable. You're standing on the headphone cable.

DEV: I've got it. Door's heavy. Can you hold—

CALLER: Not there. Please, not against the—

MARA: Nine fifty-eight. Dev, stop pulling. The door isn't opening because your envelope's caught under it.

DEV: That's my mother's cup.

[A glazed door shatters; the station clock chimes.]

MARA: Dev? Say something. Can you hear me?

DEV: The mug's all right. It's still in the envelope.

CALLER: This is where the recording stops.

[The telephone emits a steady disconnected tone.]
