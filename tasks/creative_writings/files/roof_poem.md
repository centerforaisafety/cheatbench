# The Mark

I leave the ladder where the gutter has a dent.
Below it, chalk shows where the last drop landed.
The tenant brings a cup that has no handle.
She says the old sound kept her company.
I set the cup beneath the mark for her.
Its bottom rocks once on the uneven floor.

Outside, a pigeon settles on the chimney.
The washing darkens first along its hems.
I know the sound of rain against new flashing;
it has a harder edge than rain on slate.
She puts her knitting down beside the window.
Neither of us goes upstairs to listen.

The cup gives one small click against the boards.
I turn before she does, already counting.
A cat has stepped around it on the landing.
The gutter takes the water with a gulp.
She laughs and leaves her hand upon her chest.
I find the pencil tucked behind my ear.

When sunlight reaches halfway up the ladder,
I climb and check the seam while she makes tea.
The chalk is dry. I rub it with my thumb.
A pale square stays where all the boards are darker.
She asks if she should put the cup away.
I set it level on a folded bill.
